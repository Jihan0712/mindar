What we make, and why it keeps moving.

A garment is finished the moment it leaves the factory. Everything about it — the print, the message, the moment it belonged to — is fixed at that point and stays fixed for as long as the shirt survives. Culture does not work that way. It moves weekly.

We build the two halves separately. The garment is made once, properly, from heavyweight cotton that is meant to outlast the season. The layer it carries is made continuously, and can be replaced without anyone touching the shirt.

What that means in practice is that a piece bought in the first drop is not a piece from the first drop any more. It is whatever we have put behind it since. The object stayed still. The thing it points at did not.

Drop 04 was shot in one afternoon in a car park in Vienna. The layer behind it has been replaced twice since.

The print is the marker

There is no QR patch stitched into the hem and no code printed on a label. The artwork on the garment is the thing the camera reads, which means nothing had to be added to the design to make it work.
